Platform scale · Meta

Build the platform. Build the operating model.

Growing a small QoE lab into a shared benchmarking platform—with the reliability, ownership, and product discipline to sustain it.

The starting point

The QoE lab began as a small operation supporting testing under real-world network conditions. Product teams needed dependable benchmarking, but growing demand required more than additional devices: it required a platform with clear priorities, reliable operations, and accountable owners.

What I owned

I owned the roadmap, stakeholder alignment, governance, and capacity planning. Workstream leads owned execution within their streams. Together, we grew a two-person lab into a 36+ person function across eight workstreams.

The operating model

I started with the problem and the expected product value, then translated that into a roadmap with dated milestones. As the lab grew, I added clear responsibilities, service expectations, stakeholder reviews, and an escalation path.

Automation was introduced workstream by workstream rather than as one high-risk cutover. Execution, monitoring, and reporting became repeatable, with people focused on exceptions.

I also applied product discipline to the internal platform: quarterly priorities, versioned releases, and investment decisions tied to adoption, reliability, or cost. An open-ended service still needed a roadmap—and a way to stop work that no longer earned its place.

Why it mattered

Product and engineering teams gained a dependable place to investigate quality and performance before launch. Capacity, service reliability, and decision-ready results were managed together rather than treated as separate projects.

What I took forward

Governance has to grow with the team, not after it. Capacity is only useful when people can depend on it—and a platform with no end date still needs clear priorities.
