Research infrastructure · Meta

Make the risk clear. Make the decision possible.

Turning an ambiguous remote-access need for Project Aria into an approved plan and three delivered milestones.

Senior Technical Program ManagerProgram ownership · Cross-functional delivery

The challenge

Researchers working on Project Aria needed secure remote access to a restricted research cluster. There was no established process or approved path. Security, network, and research stakeholders each needed confidence before the program could move forward.

What I owned

I developed the cross-functional program plan and translated engineering and security considerations into a decision brief for directors and sponsors. My role was to make the risks, options, ownership, and approval decisions clear—not to claim ownership of the technical design.

How I made it actionable

I put risk in the first draft: data-handling boundaries, device authentication, and the response to a credential leak. These were approval criteria from the beginning, not an appendix added after the plan was written.

I converted the technical discussion into a one-page brief so sponsors could make an informed decision without having to conduct a network design review.

Device certificates

Issued to establish authenticated device access.

Connectivity validation

Confirmed the access path worked.

Live demonstration

Delivered at the Reality Labs Symposium.

The outcome

All three milestones landed: device certificates issued, connectivity validated, and the live demonstration delivered. The network continued to support the work as device count grew.

What I took forward

“If they can’t decide, I haven’t translated it.” An approval request should make the decision, options, and risks explicit—not ask the reader to reconstruct them from a technical design.
