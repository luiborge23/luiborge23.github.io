AI-enabled analytics · Meta

Two teams. One usable roadmap.

Reconciling advanced analytics with operational reliability through shared requirements and phased delivery.

The competing needs

Data Science needed room for advanced analysis. Engineering needed a stable tool it could operate without constant intervention. Treating those needs as an either-or decision kept the program from moving forward.

What I owned

I translated both teams’ expectations into a common requirements set, compared delivery options, and helped the leads build a shared roadmap. My responsibility was to make the trade-offs and sequence explicit—not to choose a winning team.

The decision

We compared three approaches: prioritize research functionality, prioritize operational simplicity, or phase the delivery. The phased approach offered earlier useful capability without closing off more advanced analysis.

The first phase emphasized a stable platform with an API that Data Science could extend. Advanced analytics followed. Both leads helped write the roadmap, so the compromise became a plan they owned together.

Shared requirements

Translate competing preferences into concrete needs.

Stable foundation

Provide an extensible platform before adding complexity.

Advanced analysis

Expand capability on the jointly owned roadmap.

The outcome

The first phase shipped, followed by self-service benchmarking access and reporting capabilities. The result was a usable foundation for analysis rather than a stalled debate over the ideal tool.

This complemented my broader AI-enabled analysis and reporting work: defining useful workflows, evaluating pilots, and making deliberate go/no-go decisions before expanding them.

What I took forward

When teams disagree about the destination, delivery sequence can be the decision that unlocks progress. Phase the work instead of forcing one side to lose.
